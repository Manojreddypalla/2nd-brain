# Bandwidth Delay Product (BDP)

## What is Bandwidth Delay Product?

The **Bandwidth Delay Product (BDP)** is the **maximum amount of data that can be present (in transit) on a communication link at any instant.**

It tells us:

> **"How much data can the network hold before the first bit reaches the receiver?"**

Because of this, BDP is often called the **"Data in Flight"** or **"Data in the Pipe."**

---

# Understanding with an Example

Imagine a long water pipe.

```text
Tank A =============================== Tank B
```

You start pumping water.

Even though water has left Tank A,

it hasn't yet reached Tank B.

Where is it?

Inside the pipe.

Similarly,

when a sender transmits data,

the data doesn't instantly reach the receiver.

It remains **inside the communication link** until it propagates.

```text
Sender =============================== Receiver

      <----- Data travelling ----->
```

This travelling data is called **Data in Flight**.

---

# Why Do We Need BDP?

Suppose

Bandwidth = 100 Mbps

Propagation Delay = 20 ms

The sender can continue transmitting during those 20 ms.

Question:

**How much data can be sent before the first bit even reaches the receiver?**

That amount is exactly the **Bandwidth Delay Product**.

---

# Definition

> **Bandwidth Delay Product is the amount of unacknowledged data that can exist on a communication link at any given instant.**

---

# Formula

$$
\boxed{
BDP = Bandwidth \times Propagation\ Delay
}
$$

or

$$
\boxed{
BDP = B \times T_p
}
$$

where

- $B$ = Bandwidth (bits/second)
- $T_p$ = Propagation Delay (seconds)

---

# Units

Bandwidth

```text
bits/second
```

Propagation Delay

```text
seconds
```

Therefore,

$$
(bits/sec)\times(sec)=bits
$$

Hence,

**BDP is always measured in bits.**

It may also be expressed in Bytes if converted.

---

# Physical Meaning

Suppose

Bandwidth

```text
1 Mbps
```

Propagation Delay

```text
2 seconds
```

Then

$$
BDP=1\times10^6\times2
$$

$$
=2\times10^6\ bits
$$

This means

**2 million bits are travelling inside the link before the first bit reaches the receiver.**

---

# Data in the Pipe

```text
Sender ------------------------------- Receiver

111111111111111111111111111111111111

<------ Data in Flight ------>
```

The longer the link,

the more data can exist inside it.

---

# Relation with Sliding Window

Suppose

Window Size

```text
2 Frames
```

But the pipe can hold

```text
10 Frames
```

Then,

only 2 frames occupy the pipe.

The remaining capacity remains unused.

Hence,

utilization is low.

---

Suppose

Window Size

```text
10 Frames
```

Pipe Capacity

```text
10 Frames
```

Now,

the pipe is completely full.

Maximum utilization is achieved.

---

# Important Relationship

To achieve **100% Link Utilization**,

the sender window should be at least equal to the amount of data that fits inside the link.

Mathematically,

$$
\boxed{
Sender\ Window \ge BDP
}
$$

If the sender window is smaller,

the sender becomes idle before the ACK arrives.

---

# Example

Suppose

Bandwidth

```text
100 Mbps
```

Propagation Delay

```text
50 ms
```

First convert

$$
50ms=0.05s
$$

Now

$$
BDP
=
100\times10^6\times0.05
$$

$$
=5\times10^6\ bits
$$

Therefore,

the link can hold

**5 million bits**.

---

# Increasing Bandwidth

Suppose

Bandwidth doubles.

Then

```text
Pipe becomes wider.
```

More bits can travel simultaneously.

Hence,

BDP increases.

---

# Increasing Propagation Delay

Suppose

The cable becomes longer.

Now,

bits remain inside the cable for a longer time.

Hence,

BDP also increases.

---

# Effect of Parameters

| Parameter | Effect on BDP |
|-----------|---------------|
| Bandwidth ↑ | BDP ↑ |
| Propagation Delay ↑ | BDP ↑ |

---

# Why BDP is Important

BDP helps determine:

- Sender Window Size
- TCP Window Size
- Buffer Size
- Maximum Outstanding Data
- Network Performance

---

# Applications

BDP is used in

- Sliding Window Protocol
- TCP Flow Control
- High-Speed Networks
- Satellite Communication
- Long-Distance Fiber Links
