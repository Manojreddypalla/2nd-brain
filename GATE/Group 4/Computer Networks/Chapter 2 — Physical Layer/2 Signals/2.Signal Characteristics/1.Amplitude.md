# Topic: Amplitude (GATE Notes)

Amplitude is one of the **three fundamental properties of a signal**.

The other two are:

- Frequency (f)
- Phase (φ)

Understanding amplitude is essential for:

- Analog Signals
- Digital Signals
- Modulation
- Signal Power
- Noise
- Communication Systems

---

# 2. Definition

**Amplitude** is the **maximum value (or strength)** of a signal measured from its reference (zero) level.

In simple words,

> **Amplitude is the height of a signal.**

---

# 3. Graph

```text
Amplitude (V)

 +5V |            ▲
     |           / \
     |          /   \
  0V |---------/-----\-----------> Time
     |        /       \
     |       /         \
 -5V |______▼___________▼_____
```

Maximum value = **+5V**

Minimum value = **−5V**

Therefore,

**Amplitude = 5V**

---

# 4. Mathematical Representation

For a sinusoidal signal,

$$
x(t)=A\sin(2\pi ft+\phi)
$$

where,

- **A** = Amplitude
- **f** = Frequency
- **φ** = Phase
- **t** = Time

---

# 5. Units

Amplitude depends on the signal type.

It can be measured in:

- Volts (V)
- Amperes (A)
- Watts (W)
- Pressure (Pa)
- Sound Intensity

In Computer Networks, it is usually represented in **Volts (V)**.

---

# 6. Amplitude in Analog Signals

Analog signals can have **infinitely many amplitude values**.

Example

```text
1.1V

1.12V

1.125V

1.1256V

...
```

The amplitude changes continuously.

---

# 7. Amplitude in Digital Signals

Digital signals have only **finite amplitude levels**.

Example

```text
Logic 1 → 5V

Logic 0 → 0V
```

Graph

```text
5V ┌───────┐
   │       │
0V └───────┴────────────► Time
```

Only fixed voltage levels are allowed.

---

# 8. Amplitude vs Frequency

Many students confuse these.

| Amplitude | Frequency |
|------------|-----------|
| Height of the wave | Number of cycles per second |
| Determines signal strength | Determines how fast the wave oscillates |
| Unit: Volt (V) | Unit: Hertz (Hz) |

---

# 9. Effect of Increasing Amplitude

If amplitude increases:

✔ Signal strength increases

✔ Signal power increases

✔ Loudness (for sound) increases

✔ Brightness (for light) increases

Frequency **does not change**.

---

# 10. Amplitude and Power

Signal power is proportional to the **square of amplitude**.

$$
P \propto A^2
$$

If amplitude doubles,

```text
Amplitude = 2A

↓

Power = 4P
```

This is an important GATE concept.

# GATE Corner ⭐

## Definition

**Amplitude** is the **maximum value of a signal measured from its zero (reference) level**.

---

## Formula

For a sinusoidal signal,

$$
x(t)=A\sin(2\pi ft+\phi)
$$

where **A** is the amplitude.

---

## Power Relation

$$
P \propto A^2
$$

---

## Key Points

- Amplitude = Height of the wave
- Determines signal strength
- Measured from the zero/reference level
- Unit is usually **Volt (V)**
- Increasing amplitude increases power
- Amplitude does **not** affect frequency

---

## Common GATE Traps

❌ Higher amplitude means higher frequency.

✔ Higher amplitude only means a stronger signal.

---

❌ Amplitude changes the speed of transmission.

✔ Transmission speed depends on the medium, not amplitude.

---

❌ Digital signals have continuously varying amplitude.

✔ Digital signals have a **finite number of amplitude levels**.
