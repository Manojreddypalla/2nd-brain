# 2.4.3.5 Differential Manchester Encoding ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

## What is Differential Manchester Encoding?

**Differential Manchester Encoding** is an improved version of Manchester Encoding.

Just like Manchester:

- ✅ There is **always one transition in the middle of every bit**.
    
- ✅ It is **self-clocking**.
    
- ✅ It has **no DC component**.
    

The difference is **how 0 and 1 are represented.**

---

## Main Idea

In Manchester:

> The **direction of the middle transition** tells us whether it's a 0 or 1.

In Differential Manchester:

> The **presence or absence of a transition at the beginning of the bit** tells us whether it's a 0 or 1.

The middle transition is **always present** for clock synchronization.

---

## Encoding Rule

|Bit|Beginning of Bit|Middle of Bit|
|---|---|---|
|**0**|Transition|Always Transition|
|**1**|No Transition|Always Transition|

> Some books reverse 0 and 1. **GATE will specify the convention if needed.** The important concept is understanding that **the beginning of the bit carries the information, while the middle transition is always for synchronization.**

---

## Example

Suppose the binary data is:

```text
1 0 1
```

Let's assume the signal starts at **High**.

### Bit 1

- No transition at the beginning.
    
- Transition at the middle.
    

### Bit 0

- Transition at the beginning.
    
- Transition at the middle.
    

### Bit 1

- No transition at the beginning.
    
- Transition at the middle.
    

Notice that **every bit still has a middle transition**, just like Manchester.

---

# Why is it called "Differential"?

Because it doesn't care about the **absolute voltage level**.

It only checks:

> **"Did the signal change or not?"**

This makes it more resistant to polarity inversion.

Example:

If someone accidentally swaps the wires,

High ↔ Low

Manchester may decode incorrectly.

Differential Manchester still works because it only looks for **changes**, not voltage levels.

---

# Characteristics

## Advantages

### Excellent Synchronization

Every bit contains a middle transition.

---

### No DC Component

Average voltage remains close to zero.

---

### Better Noise Immunity

Insensitive to polarity reversal.

---

### Reliable Transmission

Suitable for long-distance communication.

---

## Disadvantages

### High Bandwidth

Like Manchester,

it requires approximately **twice the bandwidth** of NRZ.

---

### More Complex

Encoding and decoding are more complicated than NRZ.

---

# Manchester vs Differential Manchester

|Feature|Manchester|Differential Manchester|
|---|---|---|
|Mid-bit Transition|Always|Always|
|Bit represented by|Direction of middle transition|Transition/no transition at beginning|
|Self-clocking|Yes|Yes|
|DC Component|None|None|
|Bandwidth|High|High|
|Polarity Inversion|Sensitive|More resistant|

# GATE Tips

**Manchester**

→ Look at the **direction** of the middle transition.

**Differential Manchester**

→ Look at whether there is a **transition at the beginning** of the bit.

Both always have a middle transition.
