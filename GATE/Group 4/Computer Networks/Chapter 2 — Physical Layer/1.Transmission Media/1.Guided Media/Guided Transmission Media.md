# Guided Transmission Media

## Definition

**Guided Transmission Media** (also called **Bounded Media** or **Wired Media**) is a transmission medium in which signals travel through a **physical path** such as copper cables or optical fibers.

### Characteristics

- Uses a physical cable to transmit data.
- Signal follows a fixed path.
- Less susceptible to interference.
- More secure than wireless communication.
- Provides stable and reliable communication.

---

## Types of Guided Media

1. Twisted Pair Cable
2. Coaxial Cable
3. Optical Fiber Cable

---

# 1. Twisted Pair Cable

## Definition

A **Twisted Pair Cable** consists of **two insulated copper wires twisted together**. Twisting reduces **Electromagnetic Interference (EMI)** and **Crosstalk** between adjacent wires.

### Why are the wires twisted?

The twisting helps to:

- Reduce Electromagnetic Interference (EMI)
- Reduce Crosstalk
- Improve signal quality

---

## Types of Twisted Pair Cable

### 1. UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair)

#### Definition

UTP has **no additional metallic shielding** around the wires.

### Characteristics

- Lightweight
- Flexible
- Easy to install
- Most commonly used networking cable

### Advantages

- Low Cost
- Easy Installation
- Flexible
- Widely Available

### Disadvantages

- More susceptible to noise
- Lower security
- Limited bandwidth compared to fiber

### Applications

- Ethernet LAN
- Telephone Lines
- Home Networks

---

### 2. STP (Shielded Twisted Pair)

#### Definition

STP has an additional **metallic shield (foil or braided mesh)** surrounding the twisted wires to protect against external electromagnetic interference.

### Characteristics

- Better EMI protection
- Better signal quality
- More expensive than UTP

### Advantages

- High Noise Immunity
- Better Performance
- Less Crosstalk

### Disadvantages

- Expensive
- Difficult Installation
- Less Flexible

### Applications

- Industrial Networks
- Data Centers
- High EMI Environments

---

## UTP vs STP

| Feature | UTP | STP |
|---------|-----|-----|
| Shielding | ❌ No | ✅ Yes |
| Cost | Low | High |
| EMI Protection | Low | High |
| Installation | Easy | Difficult |
| Flexibility | High | Low |
| Performance | Moderate | Better |

---

# 2. Coaxial Cable

## Definition

A **Coaxial Cable** is a copper cable consisting of a central conductor surrounded by insulation and a metallic shield.

---

## Structure

```text
Outer Plastic Jacket
        │
Metallic Shield
        │
Dielectric Insulator
        │
Copper Core
```

---

## Characteristics

- Better shielding than Twisted Pair
- Supports higher bandwidth
- Lower signal attenuation
- Can transmit over longer distances

---

## Advantages

- High Bandwidth
- Better Noise Immunity
- Durable
- Long Transmission Distance

---

## Disadvantages

- More Expensive than UTP
- Thick and Heavy
- Difficult Installation

---

## Applications

- Cable Television (CATV)
- CCTV
- Broadband Internet
- Cable Modems

---

# Twisted Pair vs Coaxial Cable

| Feature | Twisted Pair | Coaxial |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| Conductor | Two Copper Wires | Single Copper Core |
| Shielding | Low | High |
| Bandwidth | Low | Medium/High |
| Cost | Low | Medium |
| Distance | Short | Medium |
| Noise Immunity | Low | Better |

# 3. Optical Fiber

## Definition

**Optical Fiber** is a transmission medium that carries data using **light signals** instead of electrical signals.

---

## Principle

Optical Fiber works on the principle of

> **Total Internal Reflection (TIR)**

Light continuously reflects inside the fiber core without escaping.

---

## Structure

```text
Outer Jacket
     │
Buffer Coating
     │
Cladding
     │
Core (Light Travels)
```

---

## Components

### Core

- Central glass/plastic region
- Carries light signals

### Cladding

- Surrounds the core
- Has a lower refractive index
- Enables Total Internal Reflection

### Protective Jacket

- Protects the fiber from physical damage

---

## Types of Optical Fiber

### Single Mode Fiber (SMF)

#### Characteristics

- Small Core (~9 μm)
- Uses Laser
- One Light Path
- Long Distance
- Very High Bandwidth

### Applications

- Internet Backbone
- Long Distance Communication
- Undersea Cables

---

### Multi Mode Fiber (MMF)

#### Characteristics

- Large Core (50–62.5 μm)
- Uses LED
- Multiple Light Paths
- Short Distance
- Lower Bandwidth

### Applications

- LAN
- Buildings
- Data Centers

---

## Single Mode vs Multi Mode

| Feature | Single Mode | Multi Mode |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| Core Size | Small | Large |
| Light Source | Laser | LED |
| Distance | Long | Short |
| Bandwidth | Very High | Lower |
| Cost | High | Lower |

---

## Advantages of Optical Fiber

- Very High Bandwidth
- Very High Speed
- Long Distance Communication
- Low Signal Attenuation
- Immune to EMI
- No Crosstalk
- Lightweight
- High Security

---

## Disadvantages

- Expensive
- Fragile
- Difficult Installation
- Difficult Repair
- Requires Special Equipment

---

## Applications

- Fiber Broadband (FTTH)
- Internet Backbone
- Undersea Communication
- Military Communication
- Medical Imaging (Endoscopy)
- High-Speed LAN
