# Polar NRZ-I (Non-Return-to-Zero-Invert) — GATE Quick Notes

## Intuition

Unlike **NRZ-L**, where the **voltage level** represents the bit, **NRZ-I** represents a bit by a **transition (inversion)**.

- **1 → Change (Invert) the signal level**
- **0 → No Change in the signal level**

> The actual starting voltage (+V or −V) does **not matter**.

---

## Representation

| Bit | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Transition (Invert the level) |
| 0 | No Transition |

---

## Example

Assume the signal starts at **−V**.

Data:

```text
1 0 1 1 0
```

Signal Levels:

```text
Start:  -V

1 → +V   (Transition)
0 → +V   (No Change)
1 → -V   (Transition)
1 → +V   (Transition)
0 → +V   (No Change)
```

---

## Characteristics

- Uses **+V and −V**.
- Signal does **not return to zero**.
- **Bit value is determined by transition**, not by voltage level.
- Better synchronization than NRZ-L for long sequences of **1s**.
- Long sequences of **0s** still cause synchronization problems.

---

## Advantages

- Better synchronization than NRZ-L.
- Less affected by polarity inversion.
- Lower bandwidth than RZ.

---

## Disadvantages

- ❌ Long runs of **0s** produce no transitions.
- ❌ Not fully self-synchronizing.

---

## NRZ-L vs NRZ-I

| Feature | NRZ-L | NRZ-I |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Bit represented by | Voltage Level | Transition (Invert) |
| 1 | +V (or −V) | Transition |
| 0 | −V (or +V) | No Transition |
| Long 1s | Poor Sync | Better Sync |
| Long 0s | Poor Sync | Poor Sync |

---

## GATE Facts ⭐

- **L = Level** represents the bit.
- **I = Invert** represents the bit.
- In **NRZ-I**, **1 causes a transition**.
- In **NRZ-I**, **0 causes no transition**.
- The **initial voltage level is arbitrary**.

---

## Memory Trick 🧠

- **NRZ-L** → **L = Level**
- **NRZ-I** → **I = Invert**

Remember:

- **1 = Invert**
- **0 = Stay**

---

## One-Line Revision

**In Polar NRZ-I, a binary 1 is represented by a transition (inversion) of the signal level, while a binary 0 is represented by no transition.**
