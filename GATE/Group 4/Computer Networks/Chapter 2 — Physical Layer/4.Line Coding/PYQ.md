# 📝 Concept Q&A (Interview Yourself)

## Q1. Why do we need Line Coding?

**Answer:** To convert binary data into digital signals suitable for transmission over a physical medium.

---

## Q2. What is the difference between Data and Signal?

**Answer:**

- Data = Information (0s and 1s)
- Signal = Electrical/Optical representation used to carry the data

---

## Q3. Is Line Coding the same as Modulation?

**Answer:** No.

- Line Coding: Digital → Digital
- Modulation: Digital → Analog

---

## Q4. Name the three categories of Line Coding.

**Answer:**

- Unipolar
- Polar
- Bipolar

---

## Q5. Which encoding uses only one voltage polarity?

**Answer:** Unipolar.

---

## Q6. Which encoding uses both positive and negative voltages?

**Answer:** Polar.

---

## Q7. Which encoding uses +V, 0, and −V?

**Answer:** Bipolar.

---

## Q8. Why is synchronization important?

**Answer:** So the receiver knows exactly when one bit ends and the next begins.

---

## Q9. Why is a DC component undesirable?

**Answer:** It wastes power, causes baseline drift, and many transmission media (like transformer-coupled links) cannot pass DC efficiently.

---

## Q10. What is the goal of a good Line Coding scheme?

**Answer:**

- Reliable transmission
- Easy synchronization
- Efficient bandwidth usage
- Low DC component
- Fewer transmission errors

---

# 🧠 What to Remember Before PYQs

For this introductory topic, memorize only these points:

- Line Coding = Binary Data → Digital Signal
- Three categories: Unipolar, Polar, Bipolar
- Data ≠ Signal
- Line Coding ≠ Modulation
- Good line codes provide synchronization and low DC component

Everything else—**NRZ-L, NRZ-I, Manchester, Differential Manchester, and AMI**—will be studied separately. Those are where most GATE questions come from.
