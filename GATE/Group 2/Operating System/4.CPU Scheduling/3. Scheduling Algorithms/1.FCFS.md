# First Come First Serve (FCFS)

## Definition

**First Come First Serve (FCFS)** is the simplest CPU scheduling algorithm in which the process that **arrives first gets the CPU first**.

It follows the **FIFO (First In, First Out)** principle.

---

## How It Works

1. Processes enter the **Ready Queue**.
2. The process at the front of the queue gets the CPU.
3. It executes **until completion**.
4. The next process is selected.

---

## Characteristics

- **Non-Preemptive**
- Very simple to implement.
- Uses a **FIFO Queue**.
- No priority is considered.

---

## Advantages

- Easy to understand and implement.
- Fair in terms of arrival order.
- No starvation (every process eventually executes).

---

## Disadvantages

- High average waiting time.
- Poor response time for interactive systems.
- Suffers from the **Convoy Effect**.

---

## Convoy Effect ⭐

A long process at the front of the queue makes all shorter processes wait.

Example:

```text
P1 = 20 ms
P2 = 2 ms
P3 = 3 ms
```

FCFS Order:

```text
P1 → P2 → P3
```

P2 and P3 must wait until P1 finishes, increasing the average waiting time.

---

## GATE Points

- FCFS is **Non-Preemptive**.
- Selection is based on **Arrival Time**.
- Uses **FIFO Queue**.
- Does not cause starvation.
- Can suffer from the Convoy Effect.
- Simple but not optimal.

---

# Solving FCFS Problems

## Step 1

Arrange processes by **Arrival Time**.

## Step 2

Draw the Gantt Chart.

## Step 3

Calculate:

- Completion Time (CT)
- Turnaround Time (TAT)
- Waiting Time (WT)
- Response Time (RT)

---

## Formulae

$$
TAT = CT - AT
$$

$$
WT = TAT - BT
$$

$$
RT = \text{First Start Time} - AT
$$

For FCFS,

$$
RT = WT
$$

because the process starts only once.

---

# Example Problem

| Process | Arrival Time (AT) | Burst Time (BT) |
|---------|------------------:|----------------:|
| P1 | 0 | 5 |
| P2 | 1 | 3 |
| P3 | 2 | 8 |
| P4 | 4 | 6 |

---

## Step 1: Execution Order

Since FCFS follows arrival order:

```text
P1 → P2 → P3 → P4
```

---

## Step 2: Gantt Chart

```text
0        5        8        16       22
|--------|--------|---------|--------|
   P1        P2        P3        P4
```

---

## Step 3: Completion Time (CT)

| Process | CT |
|---------|---:|
| P1 | 5 |
| P2 | 8 |
| P3 | 16 |
| P4 | 22 |

---

## Step 4: Turnaround Time (TAT)

$$
TAT = CT - AT
$$

| Process | Calculation | TAT |
|---------|-------------|----:|
| P1 | 5 − 0 | 5 |
| P2 | 8 − 1 | 7 |
| P3 | 16 − 2 | 14 |
| P4 | 22 − 4 | 18 |

---

## Step 5: Waiting Time (WT)

$$
WT = TAT - BT
$$

| Process | Calculation | WT |
|---------|-------------|---:|
| P1 | 5 − 5 | 0 |
| P2 | 7 − 3 | 4 |
| P3 | 14 − 8 | 6 |
| P4 | 18 − 6 | 12 |

---

## Step 6: Response Time (RT)

Since FCFS is non-preemptive,

$$
RT = WT
$$

| Process | RT |
|---------|---:|
| P1 | 0 |
| P2 | 4 |
| P3 | 6 |
| P4 | 12 |

---

## Average Waiting Time

$$
\frac{0+4+6+12}{4}
=
5.5\text{ ms}
$$

---

## Average Turnaround Time

$$
\frac{5+7+14+18}{4}
=
11\text{ ms}
$$

---

# Quick Revision ⭐

- Type: **Non-Preemptive**
- Rule: **First Arrival → First Execution**
- Queue: **FIFO**
- Starvation: **No**
- Convoy Effect: **Yes**
- Response Time = Waiting Time
- Simple but gives poor average waiting time for mixed workloads.
