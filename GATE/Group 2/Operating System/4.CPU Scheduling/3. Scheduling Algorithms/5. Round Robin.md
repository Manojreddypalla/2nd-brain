# Round Robin (RR)

## Definition

**Round Robin (RR)** is a **preemptive CPU scheduling algorithm** in which each process is allocated the CPU for a fixed amount of time called the **Time Quantum (Time Slice)**.

If a process does not finish within its Time Quantum, it is **preempted** and moved to the end of the Ready Queue.

---

## How Round Robin Works

1. Processes are placed in the **Ready Queue**.
2. The first process gets the CPU for one **Time Quantum (TQ)**.
3. If the process finishes within the Time Quantum, it leaves the system.
4. Otherwise, it is preempted and placed at the end of the Ready Queue.
5. The scheduler repeats this cycle until all processes complete.

---

## Time Quantum (TQ)

The **Time Quantum (TQ)** is the maximum amount of CPU time a process can use before being preempted.

- Small TQ → More context switches.
- Large TQ → Behaves like FCFS.

> Choosing an appropriate Time Quantum is important for system performance.

---

## Characteristics

- **Preemptive** scheduling algorithm.
- Uses a **FIFO Ready Queue**.
- Every process gets an equal share of CPU time.
- Designed for **Time-Sharing Systems**.

---

## Advantages

- Fair scheduling; every process gets a chance to execute.
- Good Response Time.
- Prevents starvation.
- Suitable for interactive systems.

---

## Disadvantages

- Frequent context switching increases overhead.
- Performance depends on the chosen Time Quantum.
- Large Time Quantum reduces RR to FCFS.

---

## Time Quantum Selection

### Very Small Time Quantum

- Excellent Response Time.
- High Context Switching Overhead.
- Lower CPU efficiency.

### Very Large Time Quantum

- Very few Context Switches.
- Poor Response Time.
- Behaves almost like FCFS.

---

## Time Complexity

- Scheduling Decision: **O(1)**
- Context Switching overhead depends on the Time Quantum.

---

## Suitable For

- Time-Sharing Systems.
- Interactive Operating Systems.
- Multi-user environments.

---

## Not Suitable For

- Batch systems where minimizing Waiting Time is the primary goal.

---

## GATE Points ⭐

- Round Robin is **Preemptive**.
- Uses a fixed **Time Quantum (TQ)**.
- Uses a **FIFO Ready Queue**.
- Every process gets equal CPU time.
- Does **not** cause starvation.
- Small TQ → More Context Switching.
- Large TQ → Behaves like FCFS.

# Example Problem

## Given

> **Time Quantum (TQ) = 2 ms**

| Process | Arrival Time (AT) | Burst Time (BT) |
|---------|------------------:|----------------:|
| P1 | 0 | 5 |
| P2 | 1 | 4 |
| P3 | 2 | 2 |

---

## Step 1: Draw the Gantt Chart

Execution sequence:

- **0–2:** P1 (Remaining = 3)
- **2–4:** P2 (Remaining = 2)
- **4–6:** P3 (Completes)
- **6–8:** P1 (Remaining = 1)
- **8–10:** P2 (Completes)
- **10–11:** P1 (Completes)

### Gantt Chart

```text
0     2     4     6     8     10    11
|-----|-----|-----|-----|------|-----|
  P1    P2    P3    P1     P2     P1
```

---

## Step 2: Completion Time (CT)

| Process | CT |
|---------|---:|
| P1 | 11 |
| P2 | 10 |
| P3 | 6 |

---

## Step 3: Turnaround Time (TAT)

$$
TAT = CT - AT
$$

| Process | Calculation | TAT |
|---------|-------------|----:|
| P1 | 11 − 0 | 11 |
| P2 | 10 − 1 | 9 |
| P3 | 6 − 2 | 4 |

---

## Step 4: Waiting Time (WT)

$$
WT = TAT - BT
$$

| Process | Calculation | WT |
|---------|-------------|---:|
| P1 | 11 − 5 | 6 |
| P2 | 9 − 4 | 5 |
| P3 | 4 − 2 | 2 |

---

## Step 5: Response Time (RT)

Response Time = **First CPU Allocation − Arrival Time**

| Process | First Start | RT |
|---------|------------:|---:|
| P1 | 0 | 0 |
| P2 | 2 | 1 |
| P3 | 4 | 2 |

---

## Average Waiting Time

$$
\frac{6+5+2}{3}
=
4.33\text{ ms}
$$

---

## Average Turnaround Time

$$
\frac{11+9+4}{3}
=
8\text{ ms}
$$

---

# Quick Revision ⭐

- Type: **Preemptive**
- Selection: **FIFO Queue**
- Uses: **Time Quantum (TQ)**
- Starvation: **No**
- Fairness: **High**
- Small TQ → More Context Switching.
- Large TQ → Behaves like FCFS.
- Best suited for **Time-Sharing Systems**.
