# Shortest Job First (SJF)

## Definition

**Shortest Job First (SJF)** is a CPU scheduling algorithm in which the **process with the smallest CPU Burst Time (BT)** is selected for execution first.

It aims to **minimize the average Waiting Time**.

---

## How SJF Works

1. Processes enter the **Ready Queue**.
2. Among all the ready processes, the one with the **shortest Burst Time** is selected.
3. Once selected, it executes until completion.
4. The scheduler repeats the same process for the remaining processes.

---

## Characteristics

- **Non-Preemptive** scheduling algorithm.
- Selection is based on **Burst Time (BT)**.
- If two processes have the same Burst Time, **FCFS** (Arrival Time) is usually used to break the tie.
- Gives the **minimum average Waiting Time** among all non-preemptive scheduling algorithms.

---

## Advantages

- Produces the minimum average Waiting Time.
- Improves average Turnaround Time.
- Suitable for batch systems where Burst Time is known.

---

## Disadvantages

- Requires knowing or estimating the Burst Time in advance.
- Long processes may wait for a very long time.
- Can lead to **Starvation** if short jobs keep arriving.

---

## Starvation

In SJF, a long process may never get the CPU if shorter processes continuously arrive.

This indefinite waiting is called **Starvation**.

---

## Time Complexity

- Simple implementation: **O(n)** (searching for the shortest Burst Time)
- Efficient implementation using a priority queue: **O(log n)** for selection

---

## Suitable For

- Batch Processing Systems
- Workloads where Burst Time can be estimated.

---

## Not Suitable For

- Interactive systems
- Real-time systems
- Situations where Burst Time is unknown.

---

## GATE Points ⭐

- SJF stands for **Shortest Job First**.
- It is a **Non-Preemptive** scheduling algorithm.
- Selection is based on **Shortest Burst Time**.
- Gives the **minimum average Waiting Time**.
- May cause **Starvation**.
- If Burst Times are equal, FCFS is generally used.

# Example Problem

## Given

| Process | Arrival Time (AT) | Burst Time (BT) |
|---------|------------------:|----------------:|
| P1 | 0 | 7 |
| P2 | 2 | 4 |
| P3 | 4 | 1 |
| P4 | 5 | 4 |

---

## Step 1: Draw the Gantt Chart

At **0 ms**, only **P1** has arrived, so it starts first.

After P1 finishes at **7 ms**, the ready processes are:

- P2 (BT = 4)
- P3 (BT = 1)
- P4 (BT = 4)

Choose the shortest Burst Time → **P3**.

After P3, P2 and P4 have equal Burst Times (4 ms), so FCFS is used.

### Gantt Chart

```text
0         7    8        12        16
|---------|----|---------|---------|
    P1      P3      P2        P4
```

---

## Step 2: Completion Time (CT)

| Process | CT |
|---------|---:|
| P1 | 7 |
| P2 | 12 |
| P3 | 8 |
| P4 | 16 |

---

## Step 3: Turnaround Time (TAT)

$$
TAT = CT - AT
$$

| Process | Calculation | TAT |
|---------|-------------|----:|
| P1 | 7 − 0 | 7 |
| P2 | 12 − 2 | 10 |
| P3 | 8 − 4 | 4 |
| P4 | 16 − 5 | 11 |

---

## Step 4: Waiting Time (WT)

$$
WT = TAT - BT
$$

| Process | Calculation | WT |
|---------|-------------|---:|
| P1 | 7 − 7 | 0 |
| P2 | 10 − 4 | 6 |
| P3 | 4 − 1 | 3 |
| P4 | 11 − 4 | 7 |

---

## Step 5: Response Time (RT)

Since SJF is **Non-Preemptive**,

$$
RT = WT
$$

| Process | RT |
|---------|---:|
| P1 | 0 |
| P2 | 6 |
| P3 | 3 |
| P4 | 7 |

---

## Average Waiting Time

$$
\frac{0+6+3+7}{4}
=
4\text{ ms}
$$

---

## Average Turnaround Time

$$
\frac{7+10+4+11}{4}
=
8\text{ ms}
$$

---

# Quick Revision ⭐

- Type: **Non-Preemptive**
- Selection: **Shortest Burst Time**
- Goal: **Minimum Average Waiting Time**
- Starvation: **Possible**
- Tie Breaking: **FCFS**
- Response Time = Waiting Time
- Burst Time must be known (or estimated).
