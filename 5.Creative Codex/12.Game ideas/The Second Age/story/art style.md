I think this is exactly the right mindset.

**Don't search for an art style. Experiment until one emerges.**

Almost every memorable game has an art style that came from **hundreds of experiments**, not a single decision.

For **The Second Age**, I'd actually treat art direction like a science project.

---

# Phase 1 — The Inspiration Wall

Don't make art yet.

Collect references.

Aim for **500 images** over a few months.

Organize them into folders like:

```text
The Second Age

├── Characters
├── Clothing
├── Ruined Cities
├── Universities
├── Libraries
├── Factories
├── Nature
├── Lighting
├── Colors
├── Architecture
├── Technology
├── Robots
├── Props
├── UI
└── Mood
```

Pinterest, ArtStation, movies, games, architecture books—anything.

---

# Phase 2 — Find Patterns

After collecting a few hundred images, ask:

- Why did I save this?
    
- Was it the lighting?
    
- The colors?
    
- The atmosphere?
    
- The shapes?
    
- The emotion?
    

You'll probably notice recurring themes.

Maybe 70% of your images have:

- warm sunlight,
    
- moss,
    
- concrete,
    
- abandoned schools,
    
- industrial interiors.
    

That's your taste revealing itself.

---

# Phase 3 — Style Experiments

Now deliberately try different directions.

### Experiment A

Arcane-inspired painterly rendering.

---

### Experiment B

Anime-inspired but realistic proportions.

---

### Experiment C

Oil painting.

---

### Experiment D

Low-poly with cinematic lighting.

---

### Experiment E

Industrial realism with painterly textures.

Don't decide which is "best."

Ask:

> **"Which one feels like The Second Age?"**

---

# Phase 4 — The "Five-Second Test"

Imagine someone sees a screenshot for **five seconds**.

Can they immediately say:

> "That's The Second Age."

That's the goal.

Not realism.

Recognition.

# I would also create a "Style Bible"

Something like:

```text
Art Direction

Colors

Lighting

Materials

Architecture

Nature

Technology

Characters

UI

Typography

Camera

Mood

Things We Never Do
```

That last section is surprisingly important.

For example:

### Things We Never Do

- Neon cyberpunk.
    
- Plastic-looking materials.
    
- Oversaturated colors.
    
- Generic fantasy armor.
    
- Photorealistic faces.
    
- Cartoon proportions.
    

Knowing what your world **isn't** is just as useful as knowing what it is.

# One exercise I think you'd enjoy

Take **one scene**.

Just one.

For example:

> Chrome enters the abandoned university.

Now imagine it in:

- Arcane.
    
- Ghibli.
    
- NieR.
    
- Firewatch.
    
- Breath of the Wild.
    
- Disco Elysium.
    
- A watercolor painting.
    
- Pencil sketch.
    

Same scene.

Different art directions.

You'll quickly discover which one matches the feeling you want.

# Here's something most indie developers don't realize

Art style isn't just:

> "How do characters look?"

It's:

- Camera placement.
    
- Lighting.
    
- Architecture.
    
- Colors.
    
- Sound.
    
- Animation speed.
    
- UI.
    
- Fonts.
    
- Weather.
    
- Fog.
    
- Shadows.
    

Everything contributes.

## My favorite quote about art direction

> **"Style is the consistent answer to thousands of small decisions."**

Not one big decision.

Thousands.

## One thing I'd suggest for you

Since you already like:

- Unreal Engine
    
- Blender
    
- Concept art
    
- Industrial design
    
- Architecture
    

I think your next six months should include **visual literacy**.

Not "learning to draw perfectly."

Learning to _see_.

When you look at a piece of concept art, ask:

- Why does this feel peaceful?
    
- Why do these ruins feel ancient?
    
- Why does this color palette feel hopeful instead of depressing?
    
- Where is my eye drawn first?
    

That's a skill that transfers whether you end up making comics, games, or films.

---

### My challenge

Over the next month, don't commit to an art style.

Instead, collect references and make **ten tiny visual experiments**.

They can be ugly. They can fail.

Your goal isn't to produce beautiful art—it's to discover what _The Second Age_ feels like visually.

Because I have a suspicion...

The art style for your universe already exists.

You just haven't _discovered_ it yet.
