I think the biggest mistake would be trying to do **everything** at once.

You're not trying to become:

- a game developer,
    
- a novelist,
    
- an artist,
    
- a philosopher,
    
- and a computer scientist...
    

...all in the same month.

Instead, think of yourself as **building a studio of one**.

# Phase 1 (Next 3 Months)

**Goal:** Build the universe, not the game.

## Daily (2–3 hours)

### 🧠 30 min — Learn

Read one chapter or watch one high-quality lecture.

Rotate:

- Monday: Sci-fi
    
- Tuesday: History
    
- Wednesday: Philosophy
    
- Thursday: Computer Science
    
- Friday: Game Design
    
- Saturday: Psychology
    
- Sunday: Review
    

---

### ✍️ 45 min — Worldbuilding

Work on only **one** thing.

Examples:

- History timeline
    
- AI teacher
    
- Chrome
    
- Villages
    
- Technology tree
    
- Ancient university
    
- Military systems
    
- Religion
    
- Maps
    

Never try to build the whole world in one sitting.

---

### 📖 30 min — Comic / Story

Draw rough stick figures if needed.

Don't worry about art.

Just answer:

> "What happens next?"

One page a day is enough.

---

### 📓 15 min — Idea Journal

Every day write:

- One cool idea.
    
- One problem.
    
- One question.
    

For example:

> Why did books survive?

Don't answer immediately.

Collect questions.

# Don't Build the Game Yet

Resist opening Unreal Engine.

Instead, spend these months answering questions like:

- Why did civilization stop progressing?
    
- Why is the AI still alive?
    
- How does a village function?
    
- What does a child think a keyboard is?
    
- What are people afraid of?
    
- What are they curious about?
    

When those answers exist, gameplay ideas will come much more naturally.

# One rule I'd ask you to follow

Every new idea must answer **one** of these questions:

- Does it make the world more believable?
    
- Does it make the story more emotional?
    
- Does it make the gameplay more interesting?
    

If the answer is "none," write it in a **Future Ideas** page instead of forcing it into the current world.

---

## My challenge for you

Don't aim to finish **The Second Age**.

Aim to reach a point where, six months from now, someone can ask:

> **"Tell me about your world."**

…and you can spend an hour talking about its history, characters, cultures, technology, and mysteries without needing to invent them on the spot.

If you reach that point, you've built something valuable—whether it becomes a comic, a game, or something else entirely. The game can come later; the universe is what gives it lasting identity.
