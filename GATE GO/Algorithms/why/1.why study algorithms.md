### 1. Why do we need algorithms?

Imagine you have **10 million products** on Amazon.

A user searches:

> `"RTX 4060 laptop"`

The computer has to find relevant products.

You could simply check:

```text
product 1
product 2
product 3
...
product 10,000,000
```

That's an algorithm — but a terrible one.

We want something much smarter:

```text
10,000,000 products
        ↓
     SEARCH
        ↓
find answer quickly
```

The difference between a **5-second search and 0.001-second search** often comes down to the algorithm and data structure.

That's why algorithms matter.

## 2. Search is an algorithm problem

Suppose:

```text
A = [10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70]
```

Want to find `60`.

### Linear Search

Check one by one:

```text
10 ❌
20 ❌
30 ❌
40 ❌
50 ❌
60 ✅
```

Worst case:

[  
O(n)  
]

But because the array is sorted, we can use **Binary Search**:

```text
[10 20 30 40 50 60 70]
          ↑
         40
```

60 > 40 → ignore left half.

```text
[50 60 70]
   ↑
  60 ✅
```

Only a few comparisons.

[  
O(\log n)  
]

### The important realization

**Algorithms are about finding better ways to accomplish the same task.**

# 3. Why spell checkers need algorithms

Suppose you type:

```text
I am lerning algorithims
```

Your editor says:

```text
learning
algorithms
```

How does it know?

There may be **hundreds of thousands of words** in its dictionary.

It needs algorithms for things like:

- searching dictionary words
    
- detecting whether a word exists
    
- finding similar words
    
- calculating similarity/distance
    
- ranking possible corrections
    

For example:

```text
algorithim
     ↓
compare with dictionary
     ↓
algorithm
algorithms
algebra
...
     ↓
find closest candidate
     ↓
"algorithm" ✅
```

This can involve algorithms such as **searching, hashing, dynamic programming, string matching, etc.**

So algorithms aren't some artificial GATE topic.

You encounter them everywhere.

## The GATE mindset you should develop

Don't study:

> "Binary Search = this code."

Instead ask:

> **What problem is this algorithm trying to solve, and why is its strategy faster?**

For example:

```text
Linear Search
↓
"I don't know where X is."
↓
Check everything
↓
O(n)


Binary Search
↓
"Data is sorted."
↓
Can eliminate half the possibilities every step
↓
O(log n)
```

That's the kind of understanding that will make DSA much easier.

And yes — **if DSA + Algorithms is ~20 marks, it is absolutely worth taking seriously for your GATE target.** But don't think of it as 20 marks sitting in isolation; the algorithmic thinking you develop here keeps appearing throughout CS.

**One sentence to remember:**

> **Data Structures determine how we organize information; Algorithms determine how efficiently we solve problems using that information.****
