## GATE Notes — Asymptotic Analysis: Pages 42–49

These pages mainly establish **Θ notation, how to interpret O/Ω/Θ, and growth-rate comparison**. The examples are especially important for GATE.

### 1. Θ (Theta) Notation

Think of **Θ as a tight bound**.

If:

$$  
T(n)=\Theta(g(n))  
$$

then eventually $T(n)$ is **sandwiched between two constant multiples of $g(n)$**:

$$  
c_1g(n)\le T(n)\le c_2g(n)  
$$

for some constants $(c_1,c_2>0)$ and sufficiently large $(n\ge n_0)$.

### Intuition

- **O(g(n))** → $T(n)$ grows **at most** as fast as $g(n)$
    
- **Ω(g(n))** → $T(n)$ grows **at least** as fast as $g(n)$
    
- **Θ(g(n))** → $T(n)$ grows **exactly at the same asymptotic rate**
    

So:

$$  
T(n)=\Theta(g(n))  
\iff  
T(n)=O(g(n))\ \text{AND}\ T(n)=\Omega(g(n))  
$$

This is the central idea of page 42.

---

# 2. Example: $3n^2$

The lecture uses $3n^2$ to show that constants do **not** affect asymptotic growth.

$$  
3n^2=\Theta(n^2)  
$$

because:

$$  
c_1n^2\le3n^2\le c_2n^2  
$$

For example, choose suitable positive constants around 3.

Therefore:

$$  
\boxed{3n^2=\Theta(n^2)}  
$$

But notice something important:

$$  
3n^2=O(n^3)  
$$

is also true, because $n^2$ grows slower than $n^3$.

Similarly:

$$  
3n^2=\Omega(\log n)  
$$

is also true because $n^2$ eventually grows much faster than $\log n$.

So **one function can have many valid O and Ω bounds, but Θ gives its tight asymptotic order.**

---

# 3. O, Ω and Θ — Mental Model

Suppose an algorithm actually takes:

$$  
T(n)=n^2  
$$

Then:

### Big-O

$$  
T(n)=O(n^2)  
$$

means:

> The algorithm takes **at most proportional to $n^2$** time for sufficiently large $n$.

But these are ALSO technically true:

$$  
n^2=O(n^3)  
$$

$$  
n^2=O(2^n)  
$$

because both $n^3$ and $2^n$ eventually dominate $n^2$.

So **O is an upper bound, not necessarily a tight bound.**

### Big-Ω

$$  
T(n)=\Omega(n^2)  
$$

means:

> The algorithm takes **at least proportional to $n^2$** time eventually.

Also:

$$  
n^2=\Omega(n)  
$$

$$  
n^2=\Omega(\log n)  
$$

because $n^2$ grows faster.

### Θ

$$  
T(n)=\Theta(n^2)  
$$

means:

> $n^2$ is the **tight asymptotic growth rate**.

This distinction is reinforced on pages 46–47.

---

# 4. Algorithm Interpretation

If an algorithm takes:

$$  
O(n^2)  
$$

the lecture interprets it as:

> For every input, its running time is **at most proportional to $n^2$**.

If:

$$  
\Omega(n^2)  
$$

then:

> For every input, its running time is **at least proportional to $n^2$**.

If:

$$  
\Theta(n^2)  
$$

then:

> Its running time is both upper- and lower-bounded by $n^2$.

So remember:

$$  
\boxed{O \rightarrow \text{Upper}}  
$$

$$  
\boxed{\Omega \rightarrow \text{Lower}}  
$$

$$  
\boxed{\Theta \rightarrow \text{Tight}}  
$$

---

# 5. GATE Trap: O ≠ Exact Complexity

This is **very important**.

If:

$$  
T(n)=n  
$$

then:

$$  
T(n)=O(n)  
$$

is true.

But:

$$  
T(n)=O(n^2)  
$$

is ALSO true.

And:

$$  
T(n)=O(2^n)  
$$

is also true.

Therefore, when asked for the **tightest** complexity, choose:

$$  
\boxed{\Theta(n)}  
$$

not merely $O(n^2)$.

---

# 6. GATE Examples from Page 48

### Example 1

$$  
f(n)=n,\qquad g(n)=n^2  
$$

Since:

$$  
n\le n^2 \quad (n\ge1)  
$$

we have:

$$  
\boxed{f(n)=O(g(n))}  
$$

But:

$$  
n\not=\Omega(n^2)  
$$

and therefore:

$$  
n\not=\Theta(n^2)  
$$

The lecture marks **O** as the correct option.

---

### Example 2

$$  
f(n)=n^2,\qquad g(n)=2^n  
$$

For sufficiently large $n$:

$$  
n^2<2^n  
$$

Therefore:

$$  
\boxed{n^2=O(2^n)}  
$$

But the lecture's reasoning also highlights that exponential growth eventually dominates polynomial growth.

# 7. Growth-Rate Hierarchy

Page 49 gives the major growth classes:

$$  
\boxed{  
\log n  
<  
n  
<  
n\log n  
<  
n^2  
<  
n^3  
<  
2^n  
<  
n!  
}  
$$

From **slowest → fastest**.

### Mental picture

```text
log n
  ↓
n
  ↓
n log n
  ↓
n²
  ↓
n³
  ↓
2ⁿ
  ↓
n!
```

As $n$ becomes large, the functions toward the bottom explode much faster.

---

# 8. Why This Matters

Suppose two algorithms solve the same problem:

### Algorithm A

$$  
T(n)=n^2  
$$

### Algorithm B

$$  
T(n)=2^n  
$$

For small $n$, the difference might not look huge.

But as $n$ increases:

$$  
2^n \gg n^2  
$$

So Algorithm A eventually becomes **dramatically more practical**.

That's the whole purpose of asymptotic analysis:

> **Ignore machine-dependent details and focus on how running time scales as input size grows.**

### Asymptotic bounds

|Notation|Meaning|Mental word|
|---|---|---|
|$O(g(n))$|$T(n)\le c,g(n)$|**Upper**|
|$\Omega(g(n))$|$T(n)\ge c,g(n)$|**Lower**|
|$\Theta(g(n))$|$c_1g(n)\le T(n)\le c_2g(n)$|**Tight**|

### Key relationship

$$  
\boxed{\Theta(g(n))=O(g(n))\cap\Omega(g(n))}  
$$

### Critical examples

$$  
3n^2=\Theta(n^2)  
$$

$$  
n^2=O(n^3)  
$$

$$  
n^2=\Omega(n)  
$$

$$  
n^2=\Theta(n^2)  
$$

### Growth hierarchy

$$  
\boxed{  
\log n<n<n\log n<n^2<n^3<2^n<n!  
}  
$$
