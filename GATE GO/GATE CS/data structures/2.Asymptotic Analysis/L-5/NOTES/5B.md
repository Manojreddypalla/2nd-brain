# Asymptotic Notation — Core Idea

## 1. Why Bounds?

For an algorithm, we have an actual running-time function:

  $$
T(n)  
  $$

Instead of caring about exact values, we want to know **how fast it grows as (n) becomes large**.

We compare `T(n)` with another function:

  $$
g(n)  
  $$

---

## 2. Big-O → Upper Bound

Big-O asks:

> **Can we put a ceiling above (T(n))?**

Mathematically:

  $$
\boxed{T(n)\le c\cdot g(n)}  
  $$

for sufficiently large (n).

Here:

- `g(n)` → comparison function
    
- `c` → positive constant that scales `g(n)`
    
- `n₀` → point after which the inequality must hold
    

### Example

  $$  
T(n)=3n+2  
  $$

Choose:
  $$
g(n)=n  
  $$

For (n\ge2):

  $$
3n+2\le4n  
  $$

Therefore:

  $$ 
\boxed{T(n)=O(n)}  
  $$

### Mental model

```text
        c·g(n)  ← ceiling
          /
         /
       T(n)
      /
─────/──────────→ n
```

---

## 3. Big-Ω → Lower Bound

Big-Ω asks:

> **Can we put a floor below (T(n))?**

Mathematically:

  $$ 
\boxed{T(n)\ge c\cdot g(n)}  
  $$

for sufficiently large (n).

Example:

  $$
T(n)=3n+2  
  $$

Choose:

  $$ 
g(n)=n  
  $$

Clearly:

  $$
3n+2\ge n  
  $$

Therefore:

  $$ 
\boxed{T(n)=\Omega(n)}  
  $$

### Mental model

```text
       T(n)
      /
     /
    /     ← T(n) stays above
   /
  / c·g(n) ← floor
──────────────→ n
```

---

## 4. Big-Θ → Tight Bound

If `g(n)` gives **both** an upper and lower bound:
  $$
c_1g(n)\le T(n)\le c_2g(n)  
  $$

then:

  $$
\boxed{T(n)=\Theta(g(n))}  
  $$

Example:
  $$ 
T(n)=3n+2  
  $$

is both:

  $$
O(n)  
  $$

and:

  $$ 
\Omega(n)  
  $$

Therefore:

  $$
\boxed{T(n)=\Theta(n)}  
  $$

---

# 🔥 Why "Upper" and "Lower"?

It's simply based on normal mathematical inequalities:

  $$ 
T(n)\le\text{something}  
  $$

→ **Upper bound**

  $$
T(n)\ge\text{something}  
  $$

→ **Lower bound**

So the notation isn't arbitrary.

```text
O     → ceiling → upper bound
Ω     → floor   → lower bound
Θ     → both    → tight bound
```

---

## 5. Most Important Point

Don't think:

> "`g(n)` must literally be bigger/smaller."

Think:

> **`c·g(n)` is the bound.**

For Big-O:

  $$ 
T(n)\le c,g(n)  
  $$

For Big-Ω:

  $$
T(n)\ge c,g(n)  
  $$

The constant `c` allows us to ignore constant differences.

---

### 🧠 One-line memory trick

> **O = Ceiling, Ω = Floor, Θ = Ceiling + Floor.**
