## Intuition

Imagine a city with **water pipes**.

- Water starts from a **Source** (water tank).
    
- It must reach the **Sink** (city).
    
- Every pipe has a **capacity** (maximum water it can carry).
    

The question is:

> **What is the maximum amount of water that can flow from the Source to the Sink?**

This is the **Maximum Flow Problem**.

---

## Definition

Given a graph where each edge has a **capacity**,

Find the **maximum possible flow** from the **Source (S)** to the **Sink (T)** without exceeding any edge's capacity.

---

## Terminology

- **Source (S)** → Starting node.
    
- **Sink (T)** → Destination node.
    
- **Capacity** → Maximum flow an edge can carry.
    
- **Flow** → Actual amount sent through an edge.
    

---

## Example

```text
      5
S --------> A
 \          |
3 \         | 4
   \        |
    > B ----+
       \    |
        \2  |
         \  |
          > T
```

Suppose:

- S → A = 5
    
- S → B = 3
    
- A → T = 4
    
- B → T = 2
    

Maximum flow:

```text
S → A → T = 4
S → B → T = 2
```

Total Max Flow = **6**

Even though S can send **8** units (5 + 3), the destination can only receive **6** because of bottlenecks.

---

## Bottleneck

The **bottleneck** is the edge with the **smallest remaining capacity** on a path.

Example:

```text
S --10--> A --3--> B --8--> T
```

You can only send **3** units because of:

```text
A --3--> B
```

This is the bottleneck.

## Memory Trick

> **Source → Push as much flow as possible → Sink**  
> **Each edge has a capacity that cannot be exceeded.**

### Mental Model

Think of pouring water through a network of pipes:

```text
Water Tank (Source)
        │
        ▼
   ┌─────────┐
   │ Pipes   │  ← Each pipe has a maximum capacity
   └─────────┘
        │
        ▼
   City (Sink)
```

The goal is to **deliver the maximum amount of water** from the tank to the city without overflowing any pipe.
