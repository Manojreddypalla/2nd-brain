# Here's a structured 15-day sprint.

|Day|Topic|Outcome|
|---|---|---|
|**1**|What is Machine Learning? ML workflow, datasets, features, labels, train/validation/test split, overfitting vs underfitting|Understand the entire ML pipeline.|
|**2**|Mathematics for ML: vectors, matrices, probability, statistics, mean, variance, covariance|Build the mathematical intuition needed for ML.|
|**3**|Linear Regression|Learn cost function, gradient descent, evaluation metrics, assumptions.|
|**4**|Logistic Regression|Binary classification, sigmoid, log loss, decision boundaries.|
|**5**|K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)|Distance metrics, normalization, curse of dimensionality.|
|**6**|Decision Trees|Entropy, Information Gain, Gini Index, pruning.|
|**7**|Random Forest & Ensemble Learning|Bagging, boosting intuition, bias vs variance.|
|**8**|Support Vector Machines (SVM)|Margins, kernels, hyperplanes, support vectors.|
|**9**|Naive Bayes|Bayes theorem, Gaussian/Multinomial/Bernoulli variants.|
|**10**|Clustering|K-Means, Hierarchical Clustering, DBSCAN, silhouette score.|
|**11**|Dimensionality Reduction|PCA, feature selection, curse of dimensionality.|
|**12**|Model Evaluation|Precision, Recall, F1, ROC-AUC, Confusion Matrix, Cross Validation.|
|**13**|Feature Engineering & Data Preprocessing|Missing values, encoding, scaling, outliers, pipelines.|
|**14**|Hyperparameter Tuning & Model Selection|Grid Search, Random Search, bias-variance tradeoff, regularization.|
|**15**|Complete Revision|Connect every algorithm together, know when to use which model.|

### Every day (about 3–4 hours)

- **45 min:** Learn the intuition behind the topic.
    
- **60 min:** Study the mathematics involved.
    
- **60 min:** Read how the algorithm works internally.
    
- **30 min:** Create concise Obsidian notes.
    
- **30–60 min:** Solve conceptual questions (no coding yet).
    

---

## By the end of these 15 days, you should be able to answer questions like:

- Why does Gradient Descent converge?
    
- Why do Decision Trees overfit?
    
- Why does Random Forest reduce variance?
    
- Why does KNN struggle in high dimensions?
    
- When should I use SVM instead of Logistic Regression?
    
- Why is PCA useful?
    
- What is the bias-variance tradeoff?
    
- Why do we scale features?
    
- Why does regularization help?
    

If you can explain these without looking at notes, you've built a strong theoretical foundation.

---

### After these 15 days (the practical phase)

Then spend another 15–20 days on implementation:

- NumPy
    
- Pandas
    
- Matplotlib
    
- Scikit-learn
    
- Data preprocessing
    
- Model training
    
- Model evaluation
    
- End-to-end ML projects on real datasets
    

This sequence fits your larger roadmap well: **15 days of theory → 15–20 days of hands-on ML → real projects**. It gives you both the conceptual understanding and the practical experience needed to move confidently into machine learning.
